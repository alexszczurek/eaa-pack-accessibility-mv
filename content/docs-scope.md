# Who the European Accessibility Act covers

*Placeholder — this guide is being expanded. It is general information, not legal advice.*

The European Accessibility Act (Directive (EU) 2019/882) applies to **consumer-facing products and
services** sold or provided in the EU. For websites, the services most clearly in scope include:

- **E-commerce** — shops where consumers buy goods or services online
- **Ticketing and booking** — travel, events, appointments
- **Consumer banking** and payment interfaces
- **Telecoms, media players and e-books**

## What our scope guess means

When you scan a page, we look for shop and booking signals — cart buttons, checkout links, payment
forms — and label the page either `ecommerce` or `unknown`.

- **ecommerce** — the page looks like a consumer shop or booking flow, the kind of service the
  directive is aimed at.
- **unknown** — we could not tell from one page. It may still be in scope.

We never label a site as exempt. Whether a specific business is covered — including the
microenterprise exemption for very small service providers — depends on facts we cannot see from
one page. Talk to a qualified adviser for that call.

## What member states add

Each EU country transposes the directive into national law with its own enforcement bodies and
procedures (in Poland, for example, the act of 26 April 2024 on ensuring accessibility requirements
for certain products and services). Requirements and deadlines can differ at the edges.

## What a scan can and cannot tell you

An automated scan of one page catches machine-detectable barriers: missing labels, broken contrast,
buttons without names. It does not measure whether a blind customer can actually complete your
checkout. Treat the report as a to-do list, not a verdict.
